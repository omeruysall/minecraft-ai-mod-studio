# Zombie Invasion — Character Skills, Progression, Trade and Scarcity Economy Design V1

Status: User-approved system scope; detailed mechanics are design proposals. Not implemented.

## 1. Character progression
The player develops practical competencies through use, training, books/manuals and mentorship. Candidate skill branches:
- Survival: foraging, hunting, fishing, cooking, water treatment.
- Combat: melee handling, firearms handling, defensive tactics.
- Medical: first aid, diagnosis knowledge, surgery/prosthetics as advanced systems.
- Engineering: repair, crafting, electrical systems, plumbing and vehicles.
- Construction: building, reinforcement and maintenance.
- Social: negotiation, trade, leadership and faction relations.
Skills improve efficiency, unlock qualified actions or reduce error risk; they must not make basic actions impossible at level zero. Avoid runaway numeric bonuses and mandatory grind.

## 2. Injury and skill interactions
The existing injury system remains authoritative for physical conditions. Skills can improve treatment/repair quality but cannot erase a recorded fracture, amputation or other injury without the proper treatment process. NPC medical dialogue continues to read actual injury state.

## 3. Economy and scarcity
- Core economy is physical scarcity: finite loot, finite NPC stock, transport capacity, production inputs and regional availability.
- Trade supports item-for-item barter initially; currency is an unresolved optional layer.
- Prices can vary by faction, region, demand, danger and supply, but remain bounded and explainable.
- Settlement consumption and production must be accounted for. NPCs and workshops cannot create goods without inputs.
- Loot respawn policy must be consistent with the existing loot design; no infinite container farming.
- Rare blueprints, specialist parts, medicine and fuel can be scarce without making progression impossible. Provide alternate discovery/trade paths for critical progression items.
- Player-made goods can enter trade only if the crafting system produced them and inventory ownership is valid.

## 4. Markets and trade routes
Trading posts, survivor camps, faction markets and mobile traders have schedules, finite inventories and security conditions. Trade routes can be interrupted by horde activity, hostile factions or transport damage. Prices and stock update through bounded simulation rather than per-tick global calculations.

## 5. Rewards and progression pacing
Rewards include materials, blueprints, reputation, access, knowledge and safe passage. Avoid rewarding every quest with large quantities of scarce ammunition or fuel. Skill and economy progression should reinforce survival choices rather than bypass them.

## 6. Acceptance criteria
1. Skills improve capabilities without blocking all baseline play.
2. Skill effects are visible and documented.
3. Existing injury truth is never overwritten by skill/dialogue systems.
4. Trade uses finite, auditable inventories.
5. Prices remain within configured bounds.
6. Production requires real inputs and station access.
7. Scarcity does not soft-lock required progression.
8. Regional markets respond to events without expensive global ticking.

## 7. Open decisions
Skill XP model, skill caps, respec/training policy, currency adoption, price formulas, trade route scale, loot refresh policy and exact first-slice skills remain open.
